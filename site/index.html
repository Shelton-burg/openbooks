03 — How it works

Four steps. That's the whole job.

01 — QUOTE

Quote the job

Send the quote, watch it land, and when the client says go it converts straight into an invoice. The numbers never get re-typed.

02 — REVIEW

Post and review

Invoices, bills and purchase orders arrive as drafts. You review them before they post — and if something needs fixing, corrections are reversals. Nothing silently disappears.

03 — RECONCILE

Match the bank, line by line

Import your statement CSV and allocate every line. Money moved between your own accounts never touches profit or GST — the classic traps are handled.

04 — REPORT

BAS made boring

Quarter end arrives and the workpapers are already there: trial balance, P&L, contractor worksheet — everything your accountant wants, in one pack.

05 — Run it

Three ways to get going.

Pick whichever fits. Same software, same data — all three are free.

Easiest

Windows installer

Download, run it, and OpenBooks appears in your Start menu. No Node, no Docker, no accounts. Your books live on your PC and are backed up daily.

For tinkerers

Node 24

npm ci --ignore-scripts · cp .env.example .env · npm run setup · npm run build · npm start — then open http://localhost:3000.

For servers

Docker

docker compose up -d and it's running. Full instructions live in the README.

06 — The honest part

What OpenBooks isn't — yet.

We'd rather tell you now than after your BAS is due. A working beta, tested hard, still growing.

  • No payroll or Single Touch Payroll — yet
  • No live bank feeds — CSV import instead
  • No tax lodgment to the ATO
  • No card-payment integration
  • Windows installer unsigned (beta) — expect a SmartScreen prompt
  • Not accountant-certified — keep your current system while you test

08 — Questions

Asked. Answered.

Is OpenBooks really free? +

Yes. The code is MIT-licensed — use it, change it, share it, build a business on it. There's no paid tier hiding behind this page and no plan to add one. If you want to support the project, contributing code, docs or bug reports is the best way.

Why would anyone give away accounting software? +

Because the books shouldn't cost a tradesperson a monthly subscription. The founder runs a plumbing business and watched software fees stack up year after year. OpenBooks is the community edition of that frustration — free, so nobody's locked out of running a proper business.

Does it talk to the ATO? +

Not yet. There's no lodgment and no Single Touch Payroll in the beta — you'll still handle lodgment through your usual channel. What OpenBooks gives you is the clean data behind it: BAS workpapers, trial balance and P&L, ready for you or your accountant. ATO lodgment is on the roadmap.

Do I need to be technical to run it? +

No. The Windows installer is the path for everyone: download, run, done. The Node and Docker options exist for people who prefer them, but they're optional, not required.

Is it as full-featured as the big cloud platforms? +

Honestly, not yet. The core loop — quoting, invoicing, bills, reconciliation and BAS — is solid and tested, but payroll and live bank feeds are still ahead of us. The "honest limits" section on this page is the full list; check it against what your business needs before switching.

Can I trust it with my books? +

Your data stays on your own computer in a local database, with verified daily backups. The entire codebase is public — you (or your IT person) can read exactly what it does. It's still a beta: run it alongside your current system for a while, and if you find something wrong, tell us.
